# Daily Maverick · Elections 2026 graphics library v3 (Elections Hub post-elections v26)

Every graphic on the Elections Hub as an embed for articles, with its own link and image downloads. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step. v3 replaces v2 (`elections-graphics-v2/`): the maps and the ward candidates now come from the RevEngine IEC service, the service the results come from (see What it talks to). Embed codes and links are the same as v2's.

## Publish it (GitHub Pages)

The repository is `bernard-lgtm/elections-graphics` (public); the library is published at `https://bernard-lgtm.github.io/elections-graphics/`, the `publicBase` in `config.js`.

1. Upload this folder's contents to the root of the repository with its folders (`hub/`, `data/`, `fonts/`, `assets/`): `index.html` at the top, not inside a folder.
2. Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → `main`, `/ (root)` → Save.
3. For another repository, put the address it gets (`https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`) in `config.js` as `publicBase`, and publish again. On the published site the library uses its own address anyway; `publicBase` is what previews and local copies point the codes at. The library says whether anything is published there.

Copy embed codes from the published library (or from a preview once `publicBase` is right).

## What a journalist does

Pick the graphic's place and view, press **Copy embed code**, paste it into the article's HTML block. The code is one iframe tag with a fixed height (no script), the same on desktop and on phones, so it works in a dynamic article (Results by ward's height follows its width, in its style: see Fixed heights). **Copy link** gives the version's own address; **Test in an article** shows the code in a mock article column, desktop and phone.

**Interactive graphics** and **Still graphics**, in the bar at the top of the library, each open every graphic with a snapshot of it, in the page's two groups; pointing at one of a graphic's views shows that view. The library shows one graphic at a time, so only its preview loads: a graphic or a view picked replaces the one on the page, and the address gets `#g-<graphic>` for the interactive one (`#g-seats`, with the embed code open) or `#s-<graphic>` for the still one, so a link to the library with it opens at that graphic. Without it the library opens at the first interactive graphic. **Still version** and **Interactive version** on a card switch between the two, keeping the place and view picked.

**Hub anchors**, beside All graphics, has a link to every part of the Elections Hub, the hub's address with an anchor after the #, made as the hub makes them ("anchors" in the hub's script): the page's sections; for South Africa or the province picked, each tab and its parts; for the council picked (the 44 district councils too), its file, the ward picked, its ward dropdown and its coalition mode. A click on one copies its link and shows it at the top of the menu, with Open beside it. The links go to `hubUrl`, or to `declaredUrl` once the declared switch is on (see Settings). The councils' anchors come from their names in `councils.js` and `districts.js` (both made from the hub's SA_MUNIS): if the hub renames a council, make both files again.

## Fixed heights

Each graphic has a fixed height in `graphics.js` (`h`, or `hv` for a view): 780px for most, 840 for Councils by leading party, 800 for Councils by turnout, 970 for Turnout by province, 820 for Results by council, 580 for party support, 700 for Change since 2021 (660 for its turnout and hung councils maps), 780 for votes by party, 860 for the ward result with ward finder. These include the credit at the foot: the link to the hub with the masthead beside it (20px was added to every height when the credit had two lines), and on the graphics of the full count the note on the ballots over it (40px added in v3.8, see The note on the ballots). Inside the frame (`embed-fit.css`, `embed-fit.js`) the card is exactly as tall as the frame: the title, the count's progress and the credit keep their size, and the graphic takes the room between them. Maps are drawn as large as that room allows (their width set from its height); the ward map's box takes the room over its key. The coalition builder keeps every party under its half circle: where they need the room (a council with many parties, a phone), the half circle is drawn smaller. A list shows every row; where the room is short of them it scrolls in a box of its own, under a slim bar (party support, results by council, the hung councils list, votes by party, a ward's candidates). Party support under its map keeps the room of six and a half rows, so the map keeps its size. Under 560px the two maps of Change since 2021 become one map with a 2021 / 2026 switch. `&fit=0` on a graphic's address turns this off (the card as tall as its content, reporting its height by postMessage as before).

To change a height, edit `graphics.js` and publish; codes already in articles keep the height they were copied with, so change the attribute in the article too.

**Council seats** (library v3.7, `auto` in `graphics.js`) has no fixed height: when a council is picked, the library measures the graphic for it in two hidden frames with the live results, as tall as everything in it, at the article column's width (668) and a phone's (360), and the code takes the larger of the two, rounded up to 10px, so every party with seats shows and nothing scrolls. The message under Copy embed code gives the height; the button waits for it the first time. On the other width the graphic is a little shorter, and its rows stand up to 12px further apart so no gap is left over the credit (`embed-fit.js`, fitSeats). Until a council's seats are in, or where it can't be measured, the code has 820px: copy it again once the seats are in. A code keeps the height it was copied with; should the parties with seats change after that, the rows scroll in their box where they no longer fit.

v3.13: at most six rows and a half. Where a council has more than seven parties with seats, the code is as tall as the first six and the top of the seventh need, and the rest scroll in their box under a slim bar (`embed-fit.js`, need); seven or fewer still all show. The code is also `fluid` now, as Results by ward is: measured at both widths, its height follows its width between the two (`height:auto;aspect-ratio:668 / <the article column's>;min-height:<a phone's>px;max-height:<the article column's>px`), so a phone shows six and a half rows too rather than the article column's height with more rows in it. Johannesburg on 8 November (21 parties with seats, live results): 1020px in the article column and 850px on a phone, against 1860px before. `../qa/seats-coal.html?muni=jhb` gives the two heights (`&coal=1` opens coalition mode in the frames shown).

**Coalition builder** (library v3.7, `auto` with `aw: [360]`): measured the same way, on a phone alone, so the code is as tall as a phone needs (about 460 to 570px, against 720 before) and a phone has no empty room. In the article column, at that height, the half circle is drawn as large as the room allows, with the parties beside it in as many columns as the room's height needs (`.ehx-side`, `embed-fit.js` fitCoal) wherever that draws it larger than with the parties under it (a council with many parties keeps them under it). Where it can't be measured the code has 620px.

**Results by ward** (library v3.11, `auto` and `fluid` in `graphics.js`): measured as Council seats is, for the council and the view picked (Leading party, Turnout, Change since 2021), at the article column's width (668) and a phone's (360): the height at which the map is drawn at its full size (as wide as the card, or as tall as the hub draws it) with its key, or the two years' maps with their note, and nothing left empty around them; never more than 780px. The key's No result yet row is left out of it, as it goes once the count's last wards are in. The code's height then follows its width, in its style and with no script: `height:auto;aspect-ratio:668 / <the article column's>;min-height:<a phone's>px;max-height:<the article column's>px`, the phone's height on a phone, the article column's from 668px up, and in between as tall as that width allows (where the two are the same, one height, as for the other graphics). The `height` attribute has the phone's, for a page that takes the style off. Until the council's ward results are in (the key under the map waits for them), or where the graphic can't be measured, the code has 780px and is measured again 30 seconds on. Johannesburg on 6 November, during the count: Leading party 780 in the article column and 740 on a phone, Turnout 780, Change since 2021 700 and 560. Before, all three had 780: in Lesedi, Midvaal, Cederberg and Johannesburg that left up to 200px empty on a phone in Leading party, 370 in Change since 2021, and 240 in Change since 2021 in the article column. In Change since 2021 the two maps keep their size where the room allows and are drawn smaller where it doesn't, side by side at one scale (`embed-fit.css`; before, a frame short of them cut the note off). `../qa/wards-fit.html?m=jhb,lesedi&v=win,turn,cmp` gives each view's height at both widths (`&tall=` for a frame of that height: what it leaves empty or cuts off).

Its council list holds the hung councils alone, read in a hidden frame of its own (every province) and again every five minutes; never every council. It opens on Lesedi (`muni` in `graphics.js`); until the first reading the list holds the council on show alone, and the last list read is kept in the browser (localStorage `ehx-hung-v1`), so the next visit has it at once. Where the results can't be read, the last list stays and the reading is tried again 20 seconds on.

## The note on the ballots (library v3.8)

Over the credit, on the graphics of the full count, a note in small type (`ballots` in `graphics.js`; ballotNote in `embed.js`): Councils by leading party, Party support, Results by council, Change since 2021 (leading party, party support, councils), Council seats and Votes by party. There a party's votes and share are its votes on every ballot (the feed's `votes`: ward, PR and, in a local council, district; checked on 6 November 2026 against the live count and the 2021 results). The note: Results are calculated from all three ballots: Ward, Proportional Representation (PR), and District Council. Seats are allocated from the Ward and Proportional Representation ballots only.

A metro has two ballots and no district votes in its figures (Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni: votes are ward plus PR, district 0), so a metro's Council seats and Votes by party say: Results are calculated from both ballots: Ward and Proportional Representation (PR).

No note on Councils by turnout, Hung councils, the Coalition builder, Change since 2021's turnout and hung councils maps, or the ward graphics (Results by ward, Ward result, All ward results with finder): they show turnout or seats, or a ward's result, which is its ward ballot alone. The still graphics have the note too. Codes copied before v3.8 keep their height: there the graphic is drawn about 40px shorter to make room for the note.

## Still graphics

`graphic.html?…&mode=still` is the graphic drawn for an image (the library's Still graphics): no fixed height, the card as tall as what is in it, with no box that scrolls, nothing that needs a pointer (no tooltips, ? buttons, zoom or Go to buttons, arrows, rows that open; the card takes no pointer at all), the time of the figures over the count's box, from the results service's last reading (v3.9: `Figures as at` in small capitals over `14:05 SAST, 5 November 2026` in bold, the box a little smaller under it and as wide as the time), and in the credit the source and the hub's address. The sample figures and the 2021 results say what they are in the time's place. The frame reports its height (postMessage, as with `&fit=0`) and the library's preview follows it, so the preview is the image. The Coalition builder and Ward result with ward finder are interactive only (`noStill` in `graphics.js`).

v3.4: the lists are kept short (`embed.js`, "a still graphic's lists"; `embed.css`, "v3.4 · still graphics"). Votes by party has every party with seats on the council, then the rest together as Others, with how many parties it holds (until the seats are allocated, the five largest parties, then Others). Ward result has the candidates of the parties with seats on the council and any other candidate with 5% of the ward's votes or more (the leader always), the rest as Others. Others only when it holds two or more. Party support (beside the councils map, and Party support itself) is the hub's own: the five largest parties, then Others, shut. Hung councils, the list: one line a council (its name, the largest party, the seats it is short of a majority), the Provisional or Declared tag only where the list has both. Change since 2021 has its two maps side by side from 480px wide. Votes by party is one line a party: the ward and PR split under each bar (which shows only on a row the pointer opens) is left out, as it widened the share column past the card. No list keeps the hub's room for a row that rises under the pointer, whose negative margin left the credit's foot cut off in the image.

The width: 540 by default, between the article column (668) and a phone (360), both of which the library offers too. Download PNG, the card's one image, is the still at twice the width picked: 1080 for a 540 still, a social post's width, and its type reads on a phone as in an article. The library lays the frame out at exactly the width picked, whatever the window (a narrower window shows the preview smaller), so the image is always that width. The image: the graphic's whole height at twice the width picked (under 16 million pixels); the file name ends with the figures' time (`…-map-sa-still-20261105-1405@2x.png`). The square and wide formats are still in `export.js` and `../qa/embed-check.html`, but not on the card. `../qa/still-check.html?w=540,360` draws every still graphic at those widths (`&muni=`, `&area=`, `&only=votes,ward`) and lists what is clipped or sticks out.

## The embeds

`graphic.html?embed=<graphic>` with `&area=GP` (a province; none for South Africa) or `&muni=<key>` (`&ward=<n>` for a ward), and `&view=` where a graphic has views. The graphics and their views are listed in `graphics.js`; the councils and their wards in `councils.js`.

Each embed is a white card at the fixed height: the title with the count's box for that place on its right, as on the Elections Hub (Counting in red with the share of voting districts in, Count complete in charcoal while the results are provisional, Results declared in green with the day), the graphic, and a link to the graphic's own place on the Elections Hub with the masthead. The coalition builder is its own, simpler than the hub's coalition mode: one line on the seats a coalition needs with Start again beside it, then the half circle with the tally in its middle and the parties to pick; no Hung council banner and no Back to results. Its title is the hub's question, Who can build a coalition in Cederberg?, in DM Custom with no kicker over it (v3.7). Council seats (v3.7): under the half circle, every party with seats, its votes as a bar split by ballot as in the hub's open row in Votes by party (ward votes solid, PR hatched, a local council's district ballot dotted), the figures under the bar and the key over the bars, then its votes and its seats. A hung council (v3.13) has the hub's Who can build a coalition in <council>? at the top right of the half circle's box, which opens the hub's coalition mode in the graphic: the half circle empties, the parties' buttons come in under it in balanced rows (as in the Coalition builder) and push the votes down, and Back to results stands over the box on the left (`embed.js`, .ehx-sback; it presses the hub's own back button, hidden here), which goes back to the seats. The button that clears the coalition hangs under the question. The half circle keeps its size; the votes take the room left under the parties, and where that is less than two rows and a half (a phone, or a large council), the graphic scrolls as a whole instead, the votes at their full length (`embed-fit.js`, seatsBox). No Hung council banner, and none of this in the still graphic. It reads the results every 5 minutes while the count is on, as the hub does: the RevEngine results service updates every 5 minutes. On the maps of a province (Councils by leading party, Councils by turnout, Hung councils, Change since 2021) a council clicked, with a mouse as with a tap, stands up with its tooltip and a Go to municipality button, which opens the council on the hub in a new tab; a click on another council moves it there, a click off the councils or Escape puts it down. A council clicked in a list (Results by council, the Hung councils list) opens on the hub straight away. An embed never scrolls itself or the article.

Preview-only parameters (never in codes or links): `&data=sample` (with `&at=` the share counted, `&decl=1` declared) or `&data=2021`.

**All ward results with finder** (`embed=wardpick`, library v3.2) is Ward result (single) with the hub's ward dropdown, every ward of the council, and the hub's Use my location beside it (under it on phones). It opens on the ward in its address; the reader can pick another, or press Use my location, which opens the ward they are in, in whichever council that is, with a note under the dropdown saying so (and, where the fix is rough, a link to the Electoral Commission's ward lookup). The title, the count's box and the link to the hub follow the ward on show. The ward is worked out in the browser, as on the hub: the extents in `data/ward-geo.json`, then the Demarcation Board's outlines of the wards nearby. Its embed code carries `allow="geolocation"`, without which a browser refuses the location to a frame from another site; the article page must not refuse it either (a `Permissions-Policy` header with `geolocation=()` would). Where the location is refused or not found, the hub's message says so and points to the dropdown.

**Share on WhatsApp** (library v3.5), in the two ward graphics, interactive only: the hub's button in the ward box, to the right of the candidate who won or leads, as on the hub (on phones under them, over the source line; the hub has it over its ward dropdown there). It opens WhatsApp with the hub's message and a link to the ward on the hub by its anchor (`https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/local-elections-results-2026/#johannesburg-ward-12`; `declaredUrl` once the embed shows Results declared), which opens the council's file at that ward's results. In All ward results with finder it follows the ward on show. The hub writes the link to the page the button stands on; `embed.js` (`paintShare`) points it at the hub (`EH_EMBED.share()` in an embed's console gives it).

## Settings (config.js)

**The declared switch** (library v3.10), at the top of the file: `resultsDeclared: true` since Sunday 8 November 2026, the day the Electoral Commission declared the results (`false` during the count). To flick it, change it and publish `config.js` (on GitHub: the file, the pencil, Commit changes). With it on, every embed shows Results declared in the green box in place of Count complete (provisional), with the day in `declaredOn` under it (`2026-11-08`; change it if the declaration falls on another day), its seats read Declared, and its links open `declaredUrl`. Embeds pick it up as they load, within about 10 minutes of publishing (GitHub Pages keeps a copy that long); one already open in a reader's tab turns on its next load. While the switch is on and `declaredOn` is not a date or is still to come, the day is today's, so the switch always turns them. The library's own previews of the live results follow it too; Sample: results declared, in its Tweaks, shows the look without it.

**Held provisional** (library v3.15): Siyancuma (NC078) had 25 of its 26 voting districts in when the results were declared, so its graphics and its wards' keep the count's box (Counting, or Count complete) and its seats read Provisional until its own count is complete; then they turn declared by themselves. Their links still open `declaredUrl`. The list is `HOLD` in `hub/elections-hub-post-v26.js`, by the Commission's code; the Declared page on the hub has the same (post-elections v30.5).

`publicBase`; `hubUrl`, the results page (`https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/local-elections-results-2026/`), which every link out of an embed opens at the graphic's own place (All results under the graphic, the rest of a list, a council clicked on a map of South Africa or a province: `#johannesburg`, `#johannesburg-ward-12`, `#turnout-gauteng`); `declaredUrl`, the results declared page (`https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/local-elections-results-declared-2026/`), which the links open instead once the switch is on (empty keeps them on `hubUrl`); and `deadline`, `sittings`: the same as `data-deadline` and `#eh-sittings` on the hub's markup (the switch sets `declared`, the hub's `data-declared`, for the embeds). Set them here on the day they are set on the hub; embeds already in articles read the file each time they load.

## What it talks to

Daily Maverick's RevEngine IEC service, `iec-api.revengine.dailymaverick.co.za` (it must allow requests from the GitHub Pages address), for everything the graphics show:

- the results: `/local/2026`, `/local/2026/provinces/{province}`, a ward's at `/local/2026/provinces/{province}/municipalities/{id}/wards/{wardId}`, every ward of a council in one at `/local/2026/provinces/{province}/municipalities/{id}/wards` (Results by ward, library v3.6), and `/local/2021/…` for Change since 2021;
- a ward's candidates: `/local/2026/wards/{wardId}/candidates` (the IEC's certified list of 16 September, name for name) and the council's parties for their abbreviations, `/local/2026/parties?province=…&municipality={code}`;
- the maps: `/maps/2026/national.json` (provinces), `/maps/2026/provinces/{slug}.json` (a province's councils), `/maps/2026/municipalities/{code}.json` (a council's wards), and `/maps/2021/voting-districts/{code}.json`, from which the 2021 wards are put together in the browser.

The Municipal Demarcation Board's map services (`services7.arcgis.com`) stand in only when a map can't be read from the feed; the browser console says so (`[elections-hub] map …`). Google Fonts. The hub's story feeds, videos and newsletter card are not part of the embeds and are not loaded.

## How much each graphic reads (library v3.6)

Calls to the service by one embed, on first load and then at each 5-minute refresh while the count is on (every 10 minutes after the count), measured on 6 November 2026 for South Africa, Gauteng and Johannesburg (`../qa/api-calls.html`, which runs each graphic as the article does and compares it with the files before v3.6 in `../qa/before/`):

- Councils by leading party, Councils by turnout, Hung councils, of South Africa: 20 on load (the nine provinces and the ten map files), 10 a refresh. Change since 2021 of South Africa: 30, then 10.
- Party support: 1 (South Africa) or 2 (a province), and the same a refresh. The other graphics of a province: 2 or 3, then 2; Hung councils of a province 11, then 10 (it counts every province's councils).
- Council seats, Coalition builder, Votes by party: 2, then 2; Votes by party: change since 2021 (v3.14): 3 on load (the province's 2021 results too), then 2. Results by ward: 4, then 3 (7 with Change since 2021), and one more for each ward the council's read leaves out. Ward result, All ward results with finder: 5, then 3.
- Turnout by province (v3.12): 4 on load (the national totals of 2026, 2021, 2016 and 2011, each with its provinces), then 1 a refresh. Explorer: as Councils by turnout (the nine provinces and the map files), and nine more for each election before this one a reader picks.
- Nothing while the article's tab is hidden; one refresh once it is shown again.

Before v3.6 every council graphic read every ward of its council for the ward map it hides (147 calls on load for Johannesburg, and 145 a refresh when the hidden map took itself to be on screen), every graphic read the nine provinces (10 calls a refresh), and the refresh went on in hidden tabs. A ward's own read sometimes fails (on 6 November, 18 of Johannesburg's 135 wards at once, HTTP 500); the ward graphics then take the ward from the council's read of every ward.
